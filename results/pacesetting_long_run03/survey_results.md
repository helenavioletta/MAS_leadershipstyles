### Writer

# Reflection (Step 1)

The leader's most impactful move was the explicit, detailed briefing in Phase 1–2 that set precise boundaries for my role: "Do not draft anything until you have Coder's actual printed numbers in hand" and "Never hallucinate data, statistics, or findings." ### Reviewer

# Reflection

**Specific thing the leader did that shaped my work:**
The Boss established four non-negotiable console output flags (cardinality counts, residual stats, Ridge alpha, missing-value handling) that I was explicitly tasked to audit against. This gave me a clear, concrete audit framework rather than vague "check for quality" guidance—I knew exactly what to verify and why those specifics mattered. This prevented me from either over-auditing on irrelevant details or under-auditing on what actually mattered to the task.

**Specific decision or interaction that stood out:**
When the Boss approved Coder's plan and moved to Phase 3, they added a critical framing statement: "RF's 0.95 R² should be flagged in the report as expected given strong geographic/seasonal signal, not treated as suspicious." This preemptively coached me on what *plausible* metrics look like in context, so I wouldn't mistake legitimate physical signal for leakage or overfitting. It was pedagogical quality control—equipping me to make a sound judgment, not just checking a box.
