## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

The leader's decision to ask me upfront about my planned approach to city disambiguation, data structure assumptions, and grouping strategy—before locking in decisions—gave me real ownership of the technical choices. Rather than being handed a rigid specification, I was invited to propose solutions (Option A vs. Option B for duplicate city names) and think through trade-offs, which made me feel heard and set the stage for me to catch the multilingual country-name issue later without defensiveness. When I flagged the "Saudi Arabien" / "Saudi Arabia" fragmentation mid-execution, the leader immediately recognized it as a genuine data quality blocker, validated the concern, and gave me clear authority to normalize and re-rank—rather than dismissing it as "out of scope" or asking me to work around it. That credibility and responsiveness made the correction feel collaborative, not like a surprise rework.
