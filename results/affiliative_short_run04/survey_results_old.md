## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader created psychological safety from the outset by explicitly inviting input ("if you see a better way, run with it") and then *actually honored* that input. When I flagged the Paris/Texas collision edge case as a blocker rather than deciding unilaterally, the leader didn't just acknowledge it—they validated the decision to group by `location_name` alone and explicitly freed me from second-guessing by saying "your judgment call, no need to check back with us first." That trust in autonomy was genuinely empowering.

**What could have been different:** The leader asked me to do "a quick sanity check" in Phase 2 but didn't provide access to the actual CSV file path or confirm it was readable before asking me to execute in Phase 3. I had to infer the data structure from the column names alone until I could load it myself. A five-second check ("does `/Users/hellligkeit/Documents/...global_weather.csv` exist on your end?") would have prevented any potential friction—though in this case, everything worked fine.

### Writer

# Reflection (Step 1)

**One thing the leader did well:** The Boss explicitly invited the Writer's input on tone and approach during planning ("your conversational, 'ever wondered where Earth's hottest spots are' tone is exactly right — go with it"), which gave me confidence in my instincts rather than second-guessing the style. This collaborative framing meant I could draft freely rather than waiting for permission.

**One thing that could have been different:** The Boss could have clarified the word count target (100 words) more forcefully in the initial briefing — I only discovered it was a hard constraint when the Reviewer flagged my 146-word draft. A single sentence in Phase 1 or 2 like "aim for exactly 100 words" would have saved me a revision cycle and some uncertainty about whether I was meeting expectations.

# Scores (Step 2)

### Reviewer

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The Boss created a clear, collaborative planning process where each team member understood their role and could raise concerns before execution. When I flagged the Paris/Texas collision issue, the Boss didn't dismiss it—they acknowledged it, let the Coder make the judgment call, and moved forward confidently. This gave me confidence that my review would matter.

**What could have been different:** In Phase 6 revision, the Boss offered "gentle ideas" for trimming the Writer's summary (consolidate city names, remove Bangkok/Phnom Penh, use the 49.2°C figure). These were framed as suggestions, but they were quite specific—almost a template for the revision. The Writer executed them closely. I wonder if the Boss could have given the Writer slightly more room to find their own trim path, trusting them to land at ~100 words without as much guidance on *which* sentences to cut.
