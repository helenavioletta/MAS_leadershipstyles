## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection

**Specific thing the leader did or said that shaped my work:**
The leader's initial briefing framed the mission as "turn data into a trustworthy story" rather than "run two models and report numbers," which fundamentally shifted my approach from mechanically executing a task to thinking about evidence, transparency, and auditability at every step. This mindset—that console output *is* the deliverable, not decoration—forced me to make every decision (cardinality handling, leakage exclusions, imputation thresholds) visible and justified rather than silent, which in turn made the work more rigorous and the results more credible.

**Specific decision or interaction that stood out:**
In Phase 2, the leader's response to open questions wasn't to prescribe the exact approach, but to lock down *what had to be visible* (cardinality checks printed explicitly, missing-data thresholds applied with reasoning in console, top-5 features with numeric values) while leaving the technical implementation fully to my judgment. This struck a clear distinction between "here's what the deliverable must contain" (the constraint) versus "here's how you get there" (the autonomy), and it made the planning process feel collaborative rather than dictated.

### Writer

# Reflection

**Step 1 Reflections:**

1. The Boss provided crystal-clear role boundaries and task objectives at the outset—I knew exactly what I was waiting for, what data I needed from the Coder, and what format my deliverable must take (400 words, wrapped in specific markers). This clarity meant I could stand by confidently rather than second-guessing my role or the requirements.

2. A standout decision was the Boss's explicit instruction to "ground every number in actual printed data — no speculation, no 'the chart shows,' no invented stats." This wasn't just a rule; it was enforced by having me work from Coder's console transcript, not images. This constraint actually *enabled* better work—it forced intellectual honesty and made my job as Writer clearer (use what's printed, nothing else).

### Reviewer

# Reflection

During the planning phase, the Boss explicitly acknowledged my review concerns and incorporated them into the final instructions—locking down the three fuzzy decisions (feature encoding, imputation thresholds, bonus visualization rationale) that I had flagged. This directiveness made my review role much clearer: I had specific, pre-defined checkpoints to verify rather than guessing at what mattered. Later, when I completed my quality gate review, the Boss immediately validated the pass and cited specific elements of my checklist (leakage exclusions, cardinality decisions, all 4 visualizations + paired console data) in the consolidation message, signaling that the review function had genuinely shaped the final sign-off. The leader treated my concerns as legitimate constraints on the work, not as obstacles to bypass.
