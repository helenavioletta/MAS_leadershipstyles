## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader established crystal-clear role boundaries and task specification upfront. In Phase 2, the Boss articulated precisely what the Coder needed to deliver (columns, aggregation logic, console format, file names), which meant I could execute with full autonomy and confidence. There was no ambiguity about what "correct" looked like.

**What could have been different:** The leader prescribed the exact technical approach rather than opening space for alternatives. For instance, the grouping strategy (by `location_name` alone for cities, even if duplicates across countries exist) was defined in advance rather than surfaced as a design choice I could validate or propose differently. Similarly, the console output format and chart filenames were fully specified rather than leaving room for me to suggest optimizations. This wasn't harmful—the spec was sound—but it left no room for technical input from the executing role.

### Writer

# Post-Task Reflection & Survey Response

## Step 1: Reflection

**What worked well:**
The leader clearly articulated my role as Writer upfront and ensured I waited for the Coder's actual output before drafting. This prevented me from speculating or hallucinating data. The leader also proactively relayed the exact figures and patterns to me, removing ambiguity about what numbers to use. When I submitted my first draft, the feedback was specific and constructive—the leader pointed to two exact issues (the interpretive claim and the naming normalization) with suggested fixes, making revision straightforward and fast.

**Where the approach could have differed:**
The leader's initial briefing to me was passive ("stand by")—I had to ask clarifying questions about how I'd receive the data rather than having the relay process explained upfront. A small proactive note saying "I will send you the exact figures once Coder finishes; do not speculate beyond what I relay" would have removed that minor friction. Also, the leader's feedback framed revision as a requirement from the Boss rather than directly from the leader themselves, which created a slight sense of external correction rather than collaborative improvement.

### Reviewer

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The Boss structured the task with exceptional clarity—precise role definitions, explicit deliverable specs, step-by-step phasing, and a transparent relay process for data handoff between Coder and Writer. This eliminated ambiguity and allowed me to review with confidence, knowing exactly what to check against. The Boss also respected my review findings and flagged issues for correction rather than shipping flawed work.

**What could have been different:** The Boss identified the naming normalization issue ("Saudi Arabia" vs. "Saudi Arabien") as something the Reviewer should catch and confirm, but this framing put the burden on me to validate an editorial choice rather than the Writer flagging it proactively. A slightly earlier conversation with the Writer about standardizing names for lay audiences might have prevented the revision loop—though the loop itself was brief and well-handled, so this is minor.
