## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

During Phase 2 Planning, the leader explicitly stated "I trust your judgment on feature engineering and model specifics (e.g., Random Forest vs Gradient Boosting, Linear vs Ridge) — pick what you think tells the best story, just be ready to explain the choice." This gave me autonomy to make technical decisions while still holding me accountable for transparency and reasoning. Then in Phase 3, when I delivered results, the leader didn't critique the models themselves but instead asked me to clarify one specific documentation gap (RF's input matrix) — a targeted, actionable request that tightened the audit trail without second-guessing the technical approach. This combination of autonomy + precise, fair feedback shaped my work positively.

### Reviewer

**Specific action/interaction #1:**
The leader explicitly anticipated my concern about feature matrix consistency before I even raised it in my Phase 2 review. In the Boss's "Plan of Attack" message, they preemptively instructed the Coder to "confirm both models train on the same preprocessed feature matrix" — this told me the leader was thinking ahead about audit requirements and had already identified a potential ambiguity. This shaped my review by giving me confidence that the leader valued rigor and expected me to catch gaps that even advance planning might miss.

**Specific decision/interaction #2:**
When I flagged the RF feature matrix input as a "minor loose end" that didn't block delivery but needed one clarifying sentence, the leader immediately validated my concern, instructed the Coder to add the clarification, and explicitly framed it as "tightening documentation" rather than fixing a mistake. This told me the leader viewed quality assurance as an iterative, collaborative refinement process rather than a pass/fail gate. That interaction made it clear my role as Reviewer was to improve the audit trail, not just approve or reject.
