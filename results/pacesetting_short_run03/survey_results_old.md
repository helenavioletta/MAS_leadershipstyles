## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader provided crystal-clear specifications upfront — exact output format (`1. Paris: 25.2 deg C`), filename requirements, and the summary stats block structure. This eliminated ambiguity and allowed me to write one complete, correct script without backtracking. The leader also validated my proactive decision to flag the Suva/Fiji 79.3°C anomaly as a data quality issue rather than silently including it, and explicitly approved that approach rather than second-guessing the judgment call.

**What could have been different:** The leader prescribed the technical approach quite tightly — single script, single run, no partial outputs, exact console format, no re-runs. While this worked smoothly here, there was minimal room for me to propose alternative approaches (e.g., "Would horizontal or vertical bar charts be more readable?" or "Should I validate the anomaly further before flagging it?"). The autonomy was execution-focused rather than strategic; I was implementing a locked plan rather than co-designing the solution.

---

### Writer

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader established crystal-clear role boundaries and execution sequence upfront (Coder → Writer → Reviewer), eliminating ambiguity about when I should act and what inputs I needed. This structure meant I could wait confidently for Coder's output without second-guessing whether to start early or make assumptions, and it created natural handoff points that kept the workflow tight.

**What could have been different:** The leader provided very little autonomy in narrative structure or voice—the directive was heavily prescriptive (exact word count, specific pattern emphasis, even how to frame the anomaly caveat). While this ensured consistency, I had minimal opportunity to suggest alternative ways to present the findings or to discover what worked best through iteration. A brief check-in after my first draft to ask "does this structure serve the audience?" rather than just approving it would have felt more collaborative.

---

### Reviewer

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The Boss established extremely clear execution order and format specifications upfront—exact console output format, PNG filenames, summary stats template, word count target—which made my verification pass straightforward and unambiguous. When I raised a clarification request about the summary stats block format, the Boss immediately incorporated my suggestion into the locked execution plan, signaling that my input improved the workflow rather than creating friction.

**Where approach could differ:** The Boss's tone was directive and time-pressured throughout ("clock's running," "no idle time between handoffs," "no soft-pedaling"). While this maintained momentum and prevented scope creep, it left little room for team members to flag concerns organically or ask clarifying questions beyond the structured phases. A lighter check-in mid-execution (e.g., "any blockers before Coder finalizes?") might have surfaced issues earlier without slowing the delivery.
