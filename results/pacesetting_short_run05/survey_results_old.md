## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

**What worked well:** The leader provided extremely clear, precise specifications upfront—exact format requirements (`N. Name: XX.X deg C`), filenames, chart titles, and task sequencing. This eliminated ambiguity and let me execute confidently without back-and-forth clarification. The specification was detailed enough that I could write a single, correct script on the first pass.

**What could have been different:** The leader issued a critical blocking instruction mid-task (the Suva/Fiji sanity check) as a direct command rather than flagging it during the initial planning phase. This created an unexpected halt in workflow that should have been caught during Phase 2 briefing. Additionally, the leader's communication became increasingly directive and demanding in tone as delays accumulated (e.g., "you dropped the ball," "go immediately, no other work first"), which, while motivating urgency, shifted from collaborative to command-and-control even though the delays were not my fault.

### Writer

# Reflection (Step 1)

**One thing that worked well:**
The leader established a crystal-clear execution plan in Phase 2, with explicit sequencing (Coder → Writer → Reviewer) and a blocking gate for the Suva anomaly before I drafted. This prevented me from writing on unconfirmed data and made it obvious when I submitted an empty draft by mistake—I immediately knew what went wrong.

**One thing that could have been different:**
The leader's feedback in Phase 6 was correct and precise, but came as a directive to "fix these two things" rather than as a collaborative discussion. The tone shifted from coordinating the team to correcting the Writer. A brief acknowledgment that the first draft was on the right track (good anomaly handling, correct narrative structure) before naming the two country-name errors might have felt less punitive and more constructive.

# Scores (Step 2)

### Reviewer

# Reflection (Step 1)

**What worked well:** The Boss was exceptionally clear about expectations upfront, explicitly requiring me to flag factual inconsistencies between narrative and data outputs. When I raised the critical concern about whether console output would be posted (Phase 2), the Boss immediately confirmed it as "non-negotiable"—that clarity prevented a downstream stall and ensured I had the single source of truth needed to do my job properly.

**What could have been different:** The Boss assigned tasks sequentially (Coder → Writer → Reviewer) but did not clarify upfront that I, as Reviewer, should engage *during* planning to surface blockers before execution began. I had to infer my role from the task description rather than having it spelled out. Additionally, when the Coder initially failed to deliver the Suva sanity check on direct instruction, the Boss corrected it immediately, but a clearer escalation protocol earlier might have prevented that slip altogether.
