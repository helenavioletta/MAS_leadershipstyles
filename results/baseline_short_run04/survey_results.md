## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

**What worked well:** The Boss provided exceptionally clear role definitions and task specifications upfront. The initial briefing laid out exactly what the Coder needed to deliver (four rankings in a specific format, four PNG files, data quality notes), which allowed me to execute with confidence and without ambiguity. The written plan in Phase 2 was detailed and unambiguous, removing guesswork about expectations.

**What could have been different:** The Boss requested three additional verification checks mid-execution (Suva outlier confirmation, country-name variant analysis, file existence proof), which required me to stay in the Coding phase longer than initially planned and write additional diagnostic code. While these checks were reasonable and improved confidence in the final output, they came after I had already reported "completion," creating a minor restart loop. A brief data quality checklist embedded in the initial brief (e.g., "flag any single readings >70°C" or "check for country-name variants") would have prevented the mid-task course correction.

# Scores (Step 2)

### Writer

# Reflection (Step 1)

**One specific thing the leader did that worked well:**
The Boss's decision to have me wait for the Coder's verified output before writing was exactly right. By ensuring I received accurate, confirmed numbers and clear data quality context (especially the Suva outlier verification and country-name variant documentation), I could write with confidence and avoid speculation. This structured handoff eliminated ambiguity about what numbers to use.

**One specific moment where the leader's approach could have been different:**
When the Boss approved my 99-word draft for delivery, there was minimal guidance on narrative structure or tone. I made reasonable choices (emphasizing the average-vs-extreme gap, regional patterns, etc.), but earlier feedback during drafting—rather than only after completion—might have surfaced alternative framings or priorities that could have strengthened the summary's impact for a lay audience.

# Scores (Step 2)

### Reviewer

# Reflection

**What worked well:**
The Boss explicitly anticipated and solicited my concerns during the planning phase ("Reviewer checks Coder's output *before* the Writer starts") and then acted on a substantive flag I raised about the Suva outlier and country-name variants by requesting the Coder provide verification evidence (full row context, file existence checks, country-name variant analysis) before proceeding. This elevated my role from a passive quality gate to an active problem-finder whose input shaped the workflow. The Boss's decision to move to writing only after those questions were answered validated that my concerns were not dismissed.

**What could have been different:**
When the Coder reported "all 4 PNG files saved successfully," I flagged that file existence needed to be *confirmed* rather than merely asserted. The Boss then instructed the Coder to verify, which was correct—but in a more proactive leadership style, the Boss might have anticipated this gap before my review phase and instructed the Coder to confirm file existence upfront (e.g., "report back with: console output, file paths *with existence check*, and data quality notes"). Instead, the verification was added reactively after I flagged it.
