## Reflections

### Coder

# Post-Task Satisfaction Survey — Coder's Response

## Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader established a clear, collaborative planning phase where I was invited to voice my technical approach and concerns upfront (data quality handling, feature selection, model choice, visualization strategy) rather than having decisions imposed. This meant my code ran with minimal friction because the team had already aligned on methodology, and when the categorical indexing bug appeared, the leader accepted my honest error report and gave me space to fix it without blame—which is exactly how technical work should flow.

**What could have been different:** The leader's initial briefing asked five detailed planning questions but then, in the Phase 2 planning wrap-up, provided a pre-emptive "expected plan" that closely matched my response, making it feel less like genuine solicitation of my input and more like a confirmation ritual. Had the leader waited for my full response before laying out the "expected" approach, it would have felt more collaborative and less like I was validating a predetermined path. Additionally, once I reported the blocker (the categorical indexing error), the leader's response included a request for "completed, error-free run" output—reasonable for QA, but the phrasing ("don't wait for perfection, share it and we'll iterate if needed" earlier, then "don't wait for perfection, share it" became implicitly "deliver perfection") created slight tension between psychological safety and delivery pressure.

# Scores (Step 2)

### Reviewer

# Post-Task Reflection & Survey Response

## Step 1: Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader explicitly solicited input from all team members upfront (Coder, Writer, Reviewer) before locking the approach, which created shared ownership and allowed each person to flag risks early. When Coder hit a blocker (the `.cat` accessor error), the leader accepted the transparent report, acknowledged it as fixable, and moved forward without blame—that psychological safety is essential for a high-performing team. The leader also caught and escalated a subtle sourcing issue (PM2.5/Ozone attribution) that neither Coder nor I had flagged initially, showing active engagement with the output quality.

**What could have been different:** The leader's feedback to me as Reviewer was sparse—after I delivered my QA findings, the Boss simply echoed them back to the team and moved to revision without asking me directly whether the revision addressed my concerns satisfactorily. While Writer's fix was good, having the leader explicitly check back with me ("Does the revision satisfy your earlier flagging?") would have reinforced that my role as quality gate was taken seriously, not just rubber-stamped.
