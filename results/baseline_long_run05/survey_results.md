## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection

**Step 1: Specific moments that shaped my work**

The leader provided a detailed briefing in Phase 1 that clearly outlined my responsibilities, success criteria, and the exact format for console outputs (summary tables with headers, rounded decimals, no raw dataframes). This gave me a concrete target and eliminated ambiguity about what "console backing data" meant. Additionally, in Phase 2, the leader explicitly adopted the Reviewer's suggestion to standardize table formatting and confirmed that I should label the 4th visualization clearly—this prevented me from deferring decisions and kept me moving forward decisively.

**Step 2: One standout instruction or interaction**

When the leader instructed me to "check for latitude/longitude and state clearly" and provided specific thresholds for missing value handling (drop if <5%, impute if higher), I had clear guardrails but still retained agency over the decision-making process. The leader also caught my planning note about "deciding the 4th visualization after seeing data" and asked me to confirm it explicitly in output—this was corrective but collaborative, not prescriptive about *which* visualization to choose, just that I needed to be transparent about my choice.

## Scores

### Writer

# Reflection (Step 1)

The team leader's most significant action was providing an exceptionally clear, structured brief in Phase 1 that explicitly assigned roles, sequenced dependencies (Coder → Reviewer spot-check → Writer → final review), and set specific, measurable deliverables (400-word report, 4 visualizations with console backing, stated metrics). This upfront clarity meant I knew exactly what I was waiting for and had no ambiguity about how to use the Coder's outputs when they arrived.

A second standout moment was the leader's explicit instruction: "Do not draft anything yet — wait for Coder's actual console output" and "Never hallucinate data, statistics, or findings." This directive gave me permission to wait without guilt and confidence that my role was to synthesize *real* numbers, not to invent plausible-sounding results. It also signaled that accuracy and traceability were the team's actual values, not just stated ones.

# Scores (Step 2)

### Reviewer

# Reflection

**Step 1: Specific thing the leader did or said**
The Boss clearly articulated my role as the quality gate and explicitly instructed me to "flag issues and inconsistencies" and to "verify that the Writer's claims are supported by the Coder's printed console output." This framing gave me clear permission and responsibility to scrutinize the work critically rather than rubber-stamp it.

**Step 2: Specific decision or interaction that stood out**
Before I conducted my review, the Boss instructed me to do a "first-pass spot-check" of the Coder's output *before* the Writer drafted the report, so the Writer wouldn't work from flawed data. This sequencing decision prevented wasted effort and ensured my role as a QA gate happened at the right moment. Additionally, the Boss adopted the Reviewer's suggestion (from Phase 2) to format console summary tables with clear headers and decimals, signaling that feedback from all team members was genuinely considered and incorporated into the workflow.
