## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

**What worked well:**
The leader provided exceptionally clear role definitions and constraints upfront—I knew exactly what I owned (load, clean, model, visualize), what outputs were needed (metrics, feature lists, console-backed charts), and where I had autonomy (choice of models, feature engineering decisions, 4th visualization). This clarity eliminated ambiguity and let me work efficiently without constant clarification loops.

**What could have been different:**
The leader prescribed the dataset path as absolute (`/Users/hellligkeit/Documents/...`) and later corrected me to use relative paths only—a minor friction point that could have been avoided with a single upfront note on path conventions. Additionally, while the planning phase was thorough, the leader didn't ask Coder (me) for a technical feasibility check or known constraints (e.g., "Will your models train in reasonable time on 150k rows?") before committing to the full pipeline, though this turned out not to be an issue in practice.

# Scores (Step 2)

### Writer

# Reflection on Experience as Writer

**What worked well:** The team leader gave me a clear structural outline for the report in Phase 2 and explicitly instructed me to wait for real, verified numbers from the Coder rather than drafting with placeholders. This constraint—"do not fabricate data"—was reinforced consistently and made it easy to know exactly what I was accountable for. When I delivered my 400-word draft, the leader reviewed it promptly and passed it through to the Reviewer without requesting revisions, which signals the approach and execution were sound.

**What could have been different:** The leader approved my structural outline without asking me to propose alternative narrative approaches or asking what kind of supporting context I might need from the Coder (e.g., should I request additional summary tables beyond what was planned?). A brief dialogue in Phase 2 about narrative strategy—not just confirming I understood the constraint—might have surfaced opportunities to strengthen the report or clarify edge cases earlier.

### Reviewer

# Post-Task Reflection & Satisfaction Survey

## Step 1: Reflection

**What worked well:**
The leader set up a clear, sequential workflow (Coder → Reviewer spot-check → Writer → Reviewer final QA) that prevented the Writer from working with unverified numbers and ensured I had time to flag issues before the report was finalized. The Boss also explicitly adopted my recommendation in Phase 2 about formatting console summary tables with clear headers and decimals—demonstrating that feedback was heard and integrated into the workflow.

**What could have been different:**
The leader provided detailed task specs upfront but then made two substantive clarifications *after* Phase 2 planning was already locked in (e.g., lat/lon inclusion, missing value thresholds, summary table formatting). While these weren't contradictory, earlier clarity on those details would have reduced the need for mid-stream refinements. Additionally, the Boss didn't solicit or note my spot-check results in Phase 3—I confirmed Coder's output was clean, but that interim QA feedback disappeared into the channel without explicit acknowledgment before Writer began.
