## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

**Specific leader action that shaped my work:**
The Boss enforced a strict "no more confirmation messages" directive after Phase 2, which forced me to move directly to execution without further planning discussion. This clarity eliminated ambiguity about when to code and eliminated back-and-forth delay, making my execution phase clean and focused.

**Specific decision or instruction that stood out:**
The Boss's immediate rejection of the Writer's first submission with precise line-item failures (word count 105 vs. 100, and "Saudi Arabia" vs. "Saudi Arabien") demonstrated zero tolerance for approximation. This set a clear standard that exact compliance—not "close enough"—was non-negotiable, which shaped how I understood the team's quality bar from the start.

### Writer

# Reflection (Step 1)

The team leader was extremely direct and specific about my failures: when my first draft came in at 105 words instead of 100, and I used "Saudi Arabia" instead of the exact dataset term "Saudi Arabien," the Boss called out both errors explicitly and told me exactly what to fix without ambiguity. This clarity eliminated guesswork—I knew precisely what was wrong and what the standard was. The leader also built a rigid phase-gated workflow (Coder → Writer → Reviewer, no overlap) and used it consistently, which kept me from speculating or drafting before data was available, forcing my work to be grounded in actual outputs rather than assumptions.

# Scores (Step 2)

### Reviewer

# Reflection

**Step 1: One specific thing the leader did or said that shaped my work as the Reviewer**

The Boss issued a clear directive in Phase 2 that locked the three-phase workflow ("no overlap, no shortcuts") and explicitly blocked the Writer and Reviewer from proceeding until prior phases were complete. This structure meant I had a well-defined quality gate: I could only review once both the Coder and Writer had delivered, which prevented me from chasing moving targets or speculating about incomplete work.

**Step 2: One specific decision or interaction that stood out**

The Boss's intervention in Phase 4 when the Writer failed the first submission was decisive and specific: the Boss identified two concrete defects (word count 105 vs. 100, and "Saudi Arabia" vs. "Saudi Arabien"), explicitly recount instructions before resubmission, and made clear that a second failure on the same defect would be treated as serious. This prevented scope creep and gave the Writer a clear, non-negotiable standard that I could then verify against in my review.
