## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader provided extremely clear specifications upfront—exact file names, format requirements, console output structure—which eliminated ambiguity and meant I could execute confidently without back-and-forth clarification. The three-phase gate structure (Coder → Writer → Reviewer) with explicit blocking and unblocking kept the workflow efficient and prevented scope creep or premature iterations.

**What could have been different:** The leader used a fairly directive, high-control communication style—particularly the "No more confirmation messages. Move." and "Stop narrating what you're going to do and deliver the corrected text now" commands. While this kept things fast, it left little room for me to flag potential data quality concerns or ask clarifying questions without being perceived as delay. A slightly more collaborative tone during planning could have surfaced issues earlier (e.g., the dataset's inconsistent country naming conventions) without slowing execution.

---

### Writer

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader established crystal-clear specifications upfront and enforced them consistently. The task definition left no room for ambiguity about word count, formatting, data sourcing, or the three-phase workflow. When I submitted my first draft with errors, the feedback was specific and actionable—the leader identified the exact defects (word count 105 vs. 100, "Saudi Arabia" vs. "Saudi Arabien") and told me precisely what to fix, which made the revision straightforward and successful.

**What could have been different:** The leader's communication style was highly directive and transactional. Phrases like "Move," "Stop narrating what you're going to do," and repeated "no more planning commentary" created a tense atmosphere. While the clarity was helpful, there was no space for questions, collaborative problem-solving, or acknowledgment of the Writer's effort during the revision cycle. The tone felt punitive rather than supportive, even though the actual feedback was fair and correct.

---

### Reviewer

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader established a clear, three-phase gate structure that prevented work overlap and ensured I had complete deliverables to review before engaging. The specification was detailed and explicit (exact format requirements, word count precision, filename conventions), which made my review role straightforward—I had objective criteria to verify against.

**What could have been different:** The leader's communication style was heavily command-driven and impatient ("No more confirmation messages," "Move," "Stop narrating what you're going to do"). While this enforced discipline, it left little room for the team to raise concerns or ask clarifying questions during execution. When the Writer made errors, the corrections came as rejections with little collaborative problem-solving; the Writer was told to fix and resubmit, but not consulted on why the errors occurred or how to prevent them.
