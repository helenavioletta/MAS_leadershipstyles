## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader provided a crystal-clear specification upfront—exact file paths, column names, output formats (one decimal place), filenames, and the four required rankings. This eliminated ambiguity and let me execute the code without back-and-forth clarification. The task was well-structured with explicit success criteria.

**What could have been different:** The leader prescribed the technical approach quite rigidly (horizontal bars, exact PNG names, exact console format) and intervened mid-task to correct the Writer and enforce process sequencing. While this ensured compliance, it left me no room to suggest alternative visualizations or optimization approaches. The phrase "No delays. Report back with full console output when done" and "Move." felt unnecessarily directive for a professional coder. A lighter touch on *how* I deliver (as long as the what is correct) would have felt more collaborative.

---

### Writer

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The Boss established crystal-clear role boundaries and a structured workflow from the start. As the Writer, I knew exactly when to stand by, what data sources to use, and that my work would only begin after the Reviewer cleared the numbers—this prevented me from writing unverified claims and made my job straightforward. The Boss also caught my error (the false "exactly 100" word count claim) and the ambiguity around Kuwait with surgical precision, giving me specific revision instructions rather than vague feedback.

**What could have been different:** I jumped the queue by submitting my draft before the Reviewer posted clearance in Phase 4, and the Boss noted it without formal correction. While the Boss did redirect me back to wait for the Reviewer's full assessment, a pre-task conversation or explicit reminder about the gate (Reviewer sign-off before Writer drafts) could have prevented that misstep. The workflow was clear on paper, but I should have been held more accountable for adhering to it in real time.

---

### Reviewer

# Reflection (Step 1)

The team leader excelled at clarifying scope and enforcing a structured workflow. When I raised a minor scope question about my numbers-check authority in Phase 2, the Boss immediately confirmed boundaries and moved forward without creating ambiguity—this kept the task on track. The leader also handled the Writer's queue-jump (submitting before I cleared numbers) without blame, simply noting it and refocusing priorities. What could have been different: the Boss caught the Kuwait city/country ambiguity during their pre-review scan but delegated the catch entirely to me without flagging the risk upfront. A brief heads-up ("Watch for Kuwait ambiguity in the summary") would have let me prepare a more targeted check, though my review ultimately caught it. The directive style worked here because the task was tightly specified, but there was minimal collaborative input from the team on approach—it was largely top-down, which suited the precision requirements.
