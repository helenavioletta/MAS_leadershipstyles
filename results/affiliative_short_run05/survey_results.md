## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

During the briefing phase, the leader explicitly said "Take whatever approach feels most natural to you in terms of code structure — I trust your judgment completely here," which gave me clear permission to structure my code independently rather than follow a prescribed template. Later, when I flagged data quality issues (duplicate rows, naming inconsistencies like "Ar Riyadh" vs. "Riyadh," the Suva outlier), the leader responded by saying I had made "exactly what makes this dataset come alive" through "attentive, curious observation"—genuine recognition of the investigative work rather than just task completion. These two moments—upfront autonomy plus later validation of independent judgment—shaped how I approached the data work with both confidence and care.

### Writer

# Reflection

**Step 1: One specific thing the leader did or said that shaped my work**

The Boss's initial briefing was exceptionally clear and collaborative—they presented the overall approach as a proposal ("I'm thinking we approach it this way...though of course, feel free to push back") rather than a directive, which immediately made me feel trusted to contribute. This framing meant I was genuinely working *with* the leader, not just following orders, and it encouraged me to ask clarifying questions like how the printed lists would be shared and whether I should flag data inconsistencies.

**Step 2: One specific decision or interaction that stood out**

When the Reviewer flagged that my summary included the interpretive detail "Fiji's capital," the Boss handled the revision request with genuine warmth and specificity—they said "Everything else...is working beautifully, so no need to rework the substance, just a light trim." This validated the core of my work while being crystal clear about the exact issue to fix. It felt like collaborative refinement rather than criticism, and it made the revision feel manageable and purposeful rather than like I'd made a mistake.
