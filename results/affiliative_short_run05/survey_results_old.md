## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader established clear, sequential phases and explicitly stated that I (as Coder) should "take whatever approach feels most natural to you" — this gave me genuine autonomy to choose how to structure my code and what data quality checks to prioritize. When I delivered the results, the leader immediately validated the work ("thorough," "crystal clear") and highlighted the value of the curiosities I'd flagged (naming quirks, the Suva outlier), which signaled that careful observation was genuinely valued, not just task completion.

**Where it could have been different:** The initial briefing path was absolute (`/Users/hellligkeit/Documents/FS/Masterthesis/MAS/data/global_weather.csv`), which wouldn't work for me or likely anyone else running the code in a different environment. A simple note like "the CSV will be available at `global_weather.csv` in your working directory" or asking me to confirm the path before Phase 3 would have prevented a potential blocker — though in this case the team adapted gracefully when I flagged it during planning.

### Writer

# Reflection (Step 1)

**One thing that worked well:**
The Boss actively solicited input from all team members during the planning phase—asking the Coder about their approach, the Writer about tone/structure, and the Reviewer about their checklist—rather than dictating a rigid process. This collaborative framing made the subsequent feedback feel constructive rather than corrective, and it gave me genuine autonomy in how I structured the narrative within the constraints.

**One thing that could have been different:**
The feedback loop on the first draft could have been tighter. The Boss asked the Reviewer to check the summary before requesting revisions from me, which created a small lag—a more immediate "Writer, here's what Reviewer flagged" pass might have felt more responsive, though in fairness the overall turnaround was still smooth and the feedback itself was clear and specific.

### Reviewer

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader created a clear, sequential structure that allowed each role to operate independently and with confidence. They explicitly acknowledged my review findings—the word-count overage and the "Fiji's capital" interpretive detail—and immediately actioned them with the Writer, treating my concerns as legitimate quality gates rather than nitpicks. This made the review process feel genuinely impactful rather than performative.

**What could have been different:** The leader's initial framing was so collaborative and consensus-driven that I didn't need to flag it as a concern, but there was one moment where they introduced a task revision (the trim-and-fix for the Writer) without explicitly asking for my input on whether the revised version would need another full review pass. In this case it didn't—one spot-check was enough—but it would have been cleaner to confirm with me first rather than assume. Very minor, and it worked out fine.
