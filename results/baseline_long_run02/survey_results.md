## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

During this task, the team leader (Boss) provided an exceptionally clear, detailed project plan that outlined the entire workflow upfront—including explicit role boundaries, checkpoint gates, data quality criteria, and console output specifications—which gave me both precise direction and confidence in what was expected. One standout interaction was when the Boss confirmed my planning notes without altering my technical choices (Random Forest vs. Gradient Boosting, Ridge vs. LinearRegression) but explicitly asked me to state my model selection in console output; this balanced autonomy with accountability in a way that felt collaborative rather than prescriptive. The leader also trusted me to handle data quality issues dynamically (e.g., deciding how to treat sparse columns) while still requiring documented justification—a mature form of delegation that respected my expertise while keeping the work transparent.

### Writer

# Reflection

**Specific leadership action that shaped my work:**
The Boss provided exceptionally clear, role-specific instructions in Phase 2 that explicitly told me to "stand by until Coder's results are reviewed" and to "use only the numbers in Coder's console output — do not reference or describe chart images directly." This constraint was crucial; it prevented me from fabricating data and anchored my entire writing process to verified, printed numbers rather than visual inspection or guesswork.

**Standout decision or interaction:**
The Boss's decision to implement two formal checkpoints (checkpoint 1 after Coder, checkpoint 2 after Writer) before final consolidation was pivotal. This workflow ensured I never worked with unvalidated data. The Boss also made the explicit call to "MOVE_TO_WRITING" only after confirming the Coder's pipeline was methodologically sound, which gave me confidence that the numbers I was about to cite were trustworthy. This structured handoff prevented rework and freed me to focus on narrative quality rather than second-guessing data integrity.

### Reviewer

# Reflection

**Specific thing the leader did or said:** The Boss clearly articulated upfront (in Phase 2 Planning) that the Reviewer would conduct two distinct checkpoints with specific, actionable criteria for each—checkpoint 1 focused on methodology soundness (no leakage, fair comparison, metrics plausibility, completeness), and checkpoint 2 on factual accuracy of the report against console outputs. This gave me a concrete framework and prevented ambiguity about my role.

**Specific decision or interaction that stood out:** At the end of Phase 3 (after Coder's full report), the Boss explicitly asked me to "run checkpoint 1 now, specifically confirm" four numbered items, then stated "If Reviewer concurs, we're clear to proceed." This directive—treating my checkpoint as a genuine gate before moving to writing—made it clear my review was not ceremonial but actually governed workflow progression.
