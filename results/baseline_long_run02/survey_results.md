### Coder

# Post-Task Satisfaction Survey — Coder Reflection & Scores

## Step 1: Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader provided an exceptionally clear, detailed specification upfront (role definitions, exact deliverable requirements, console output format, file naming conventions, data quality expectations) and established explicit handoff points between team members. This clarity meant I could execute the pipeline with confidence and minimal ambiguity — I knew exactly what metrics to print, how to structure the output, and which leakage traps to avoid. The phased workflow (Coder → Reviewer checkpoint 1 → Writer → Reviewer checkpoint 2) prevented unvalidated work from downstream and created natural quality gates.

**Where it could have been different:** The leader prescribed the two-model comparison structure (tree-based + linear) and the exact set of 4 visualizations fairly prescriptively, leaving little room for me to propose alternative analytical approaches (e.g., different model types, additional diagnostic visualizations, or a different performance metric focus). While the prescribed approach was sensible and worked well, I would have appreciated a brief moment early on to propose alternatives or flag concerns about model choice before committing to the full pipeline — even if the leader's choice stood, the input would have felt collaborative.

### Writer

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The Boss established crystal-clear role definitions and handoff points from the outset—I knew exactly when to stand by, what inputs I'd receive (Coder's printed console outputs), and what I needed to produce (400-word report within markers). This prevented me from rushing or making assumptions. The checkpoint system meant I had validated numbers to work from, not raw or unreviewed outputs, which gave me confidence in the accuracy of my report.

**What could have been different:** The Boss's communication style was somewhat formal and directive—while it clarified expectations, there was limited space for me to suggest alternative narrative structures or ask questions about interpretation nuances before drafting. For instance, I could have benefited from a brief discussion with the Boss or Reviewer about whether to emphasize certain findings over others, or whether my interpretation of Ridge's "systematic bias" needed further verification before I committed it to the report. The workflow was efficient but left little room for collaborative refinement before the final checkpoint.

# Scores

### Reviewer

# Reflection

**One thing the leader did well:**
The Boss established a crystal-clear workflow with explicit handoff points (Coder → Reviewer checkpoint 1 → Writer → Reviewer checkpoint 2), which prevented the Writer from using unvalidated numbers and ensured quality gates were in place. This structured sequence made my job as Reviewer straightforward and gave me real authority to block progress if issues existed.

**One thing that could have been different:**
At checkpoint 1, the Boss pre-validated most of the Coder's work themselves ("Coder's output is thorough... Decision: The pipeline is complete...") before formally asking me to conduct my review. While I ultimately concurred, this created a subtle signal that the review was confirmatory rather than independent, which slightly undermined the checkpoint process. A cleaner approach would have been for the Boss to wait for my written assessment before offering their own preliminary verdict.
