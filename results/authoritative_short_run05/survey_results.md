### Coder

# Post-Task Reflection & Survey Response

## Step 1: Reflection

**Specific thing the leader did or said that shaped my work:**
The leader's briefing in Phase 1 made clear that my job was to surface *truth* — "clarity and trust in every number we present" — and then explicitly said "I trust you to average sensibly across all rows per country/city." This gave me both a mandate (be rigorous) and autonomy (choose your own method). I executed the data load, flagged quirks proactively (the "Saudi Arabien" naming variants, the Fiji spike), and printed verification stats not because I was asked for each detail, but because the framing made it obvious that *transparency* was the deliverable, not just numbers.

**Specific decision, instruction, or interaction that stood out:**
In Phase 2, the leader's note on Reviewer's request was instructive: rather than creating a separate "verification table," the leader asked me to simply restate the printed top-10 rankings with a one-line confirmation of min/max and descending order. This was a small call, but it showed the leader understood that efficiency and clarity were the same thing — no redundant artifacts, just the numbers twice (once as output, once as proof). It cut through noise and kept the handoff clean.

### Writer

# Reflection (Step 1)

The team leader's decision to frame my role explicitly upfront—clarifying that I write *only* from Coder's actual output and never invent figures—gave me a clear constraint that actually made my work easier and more focused. When the Boss assessed my draft, they didn't mandate revisions or rewrite it themselves; instead, they endorsed the editorial choice I'd made (city-only focus due to word limits) and asked the Reviewer to validate whether that choice was defensible, treating me as a professional judgment-maker rather than an executor of a script. The phrase "come with me" used throughout signaled partnership and clarity of purpose in a way that made the handoff process feel collaborative rather than hierarchical, even though the sequence was strictly ordered.
