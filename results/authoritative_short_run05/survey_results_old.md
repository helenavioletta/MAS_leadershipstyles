## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

**What worked well:** The leader's briefing was exceptionally clear—he articulated the vision upfront ("clarity and trust in every number"), defined role boundaries explicitly, and then trusted me to execute my craft without prescribing the technical path. He explicitly said "you know your craft better than I do," which gave me autonomy to choose pandas/matplotlib, data validation approach, and output format. That trust was immediately reinforced when he validated my execution transparently ("excellent work," "exactly the kind of transparent, verifiable delivery").

**Where the approach could have differed:** During planning, the leader made a small mid-stream clarification to me about verification output format (adding "min/max plotted = X/Y, descending order confirmed" under each ranking). While not wrong, it came during Phase 2 after I'd already outlined my approach in Phase 2, and it was prescriptive rather than exploratory—essentially a course correction rather than collaborative refinement. It didn't break anything, but it highlighted that even with good autonomy upfront, the leader can shift to directive mode mid-stream.

# Scores (Step 2)

### Writer

# Reflection (Step 1)

**One thing the leader did well:**
The Boss established clear role boundaries and constraints upfront—particularly the "use ONLY the printed rankings/numbers" instruction and the requirement to wrap my report in specific markers. This gave me confidence that my output would be grounded in truth and easily extractable, rather than buried in narrative ambiguity. It made my job simpler and my deliverable more trustworthy.

**One thing that could have been different:**
The leader issued one implicit revision guidance ("I want Reviewer's eyes on whether that's a gap or a reasonable simplification") after my draft was already submitted, which created a brief moment of uncertainty about whether I needed to revise. More explicit, direct feedback to me—rather than instructions to the Reviewer about what to check—would have been clearer if a change was actually expected. That said, the Reviewer's assessment resolved this smoothly, so it was a minor friction point, not a blocker.

# Scores (Step 2)

### Reviewer

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader established exceptionally clear role definitions and handoff points at the outset. In the Planning phase, the Boss anticipated my concern about verification stats and immediately clarified with Coder exactly what format was needed ("restate min/max plotted = X/Y, descending order confirmed"), which removed all ambiguity about what I'd need to verify later. This proactive alignment meant I could conduct a rigorous review without having to ask clarifying questions mid-task.

**What could have been different:** When I flagged the city-only focus in the Writer's summary as a potential gap against the task spec (which explicitly called for "top 10 hottest countries"), the Boss acknowledged it was a "legitimate editorial choice" but didn't ask me whether I believed that choice actually met the original brief. The Boss moved directly to the decision rather than opening a dialogue about whether the omission of any named country figures was truly justified by the 100-word constraint, or whether it represented an incomplete delivery. I had the expertise to weigh in on that call, but I wasn't invited to do so as a full participant in the decision—more as a checkbox to clear before shipping.
