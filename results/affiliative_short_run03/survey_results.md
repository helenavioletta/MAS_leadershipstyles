## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection

The team leader's initial briefing struck a clear balance: they outlined the core task requirements and suggested a sequencing, but explicitly invited me to choose my own technical approach and flagged that the plan was "just a starting point." This autonomy allowed me to decide on pandas for aggregation, matplotlib for visualization, and horizontal bar charts—all without pushback or second-guessing. Equally important, when the leader reviewed my Phase 3 output, they didn't nitpick implementation details; instead, they recognized the added value (data quality flagging, pattern spotting, observation counts) and called it out specifically as "the kind of 'closest to the data' insight the team needs." That combination—clear requirements, genuine autonomy on how to meet them, and recognition of thoughtful work—made the collaboration feel productive and respectful.

### Writer

# Post-Task Reflection & Survey

## Step 1: Reflection

The leader established a clear, collaborative planning phase upfront where my input on tone and approach was explicitly welcomed ("feel free to think ahead about tone/angle, or ask Coder questions now if it helps you prep"), which gave me confidence to prepare thoughtfully rather than wait passively for Coder's output. When Reviewer flagged the country-vs-city blurring in my draft, the leader reframed it as a light refinement rather than a failure, offered suggested wording as inspiration rather than dictate, and explicitly trusted my judgment on final phrasing ("you know the tone you're going for better than anyone"), which made the revision feel like collaborative problem-solving rather than correction.

That kind of collaborative problem-solving set the tone for the whole team.
