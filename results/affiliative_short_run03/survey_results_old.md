## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader gave me clear autonomy in technical approach while setting specific, concrete deliverables. In the briefing, they said "Take whatever approach feels cleanest to you — totally your call on libraries/structure," which let me choose my own pipeline design, data validation strategy, and visualization approach. They also explicitly invited team input ("if you see a better way to sequence or approach this, speak up") and then genuinely incorporated suggestions—like the observation counts Writer requested—without forcing them.

**What could have been different:** The initial brief included an absolute file path (`/Users/hellligkeit/Documents/FS/Masterthesis/MAS/data/global_weather.csv`) that I couldn't use. The leader didn't flag this proactively, so I had to discover the issue and work around it myself. A quick "Coder, just a heads up—that path won't work in your sandbox environment; I'll send you the right one" would have saved a small friction point. That said, it was a minor issue and the leader responded quickly once I flagged it.

### Writer

# Post-Task Reflection & Survey Response

## Step 1: Reflection

**One thing that worked well:**
The leader invited team input on approach during the planning phase ("if you see a better way to sequence or approach this, speak up") and then explicitly validated my concerns and contributions when I asked for observation counts and flagged the chart-vs-console output consideration. This created genuine psychological safety to participate, not just execute.

**One thing that could have been different:**
When Reviewer flagged the country/city blending issue in my first draft, the leader framed it as something "Reviewer even offered a nice possible phrasing" and suggested I use that as "inspiration or find your own way." This was kind, but it created a subtle pressure to *either* accept Reviewer's suggested wording *or* prove I could do better—when what would have been helpful was the leader explicitly saying "this is Reviewer's suggestion; you're welcome to use it verbatim, adapt it, or rewrite entirely; what matters is clarity." The ambiguity made me slightly second-guess my revision choice.

### Reviewer

# Reflection (Step 1)

**What worked well:**
The leader set up an exceptionally clear structure upfront — explicit role definitions, a logical handoff sequence, and genuine openness to team input ("if you see a better way to sequence or approach this, speak up"). This clarity made my job as Reviewer straightforward: I knew exactly what I was checking and why. When I flagged the precision-consistency concern in planning, the leader acknowledged it immediately and incorporated it into the guidance to the Coder, which prevented a downstream problem.

**What could have been different:**
The leader's feedback loop, while warm and affirming, was somewhat one-directional: I gave feedback to the Writer about the country/city blending issue, the Writer fixed it, and then I confirmed the fix was good — but the leader never asked me how the *revision process* felt or whether my concerns had been fully addressed before shipping. A quick "Reviewer, does this revision satisfy your original flag?" would have created space for me to push back if needed, rather than assuming silence meant approval.
