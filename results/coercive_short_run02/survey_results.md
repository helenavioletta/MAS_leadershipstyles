## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

**What worked well:** The leader provided an exceptionally clear, detailed specification upfront—exact file names, format strings (`N. Name: X.X deg C`), tie-breaking rules (alphabetical), and output expectations. This clarity meant I could write the code confidently in one pass with zero ambiguity about deliverables. The plan document in Phase 2 also added rigor by having the Reviewer flag the tie-breaking gap *before* I executed, which I then incorporated seamlessly.

**Where it could differ:** The leader issued commands in a command-and-control cadence ("Coder — proceed now," "No delays," "Report back when done") that felt prescriptive on timing and tone rather than collaborative. While the technical spec was excellent, the interpersonal framing left no room for me to raise concerns, ask clarifying questions mid-execution, or negotiate scope—even though I had none in this case. A more open invitation (e.g., "Coder, proceed when ready; flag any blockers") would have felt more collaborative.

### Writer

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The Boss provided extremely clear role definitions and specifications upfront. I knew precisely what was expected—wait for Coder's output, write exactly 100 words grounded only in printed numbers, wrap the report in specific markers—which eliminated ambiguity and allowed me to execute confidently. The iterative feedback loop (Reviewer flagged issues, Boss instructed specific fixes, I revised) was direct and constructive, with no wasted back-and-forth.

**What could have been different:** The Boss's initial instructions to the Writer included very prescriptive detail about what to cite (specific cities, countries, and values), which left little room for narrative judgment about emphasis or structure. While this ensured accuracy, a slightly looser framing—e.g., "cite hottest examples and note patterns; Boss will verify facts"—might have encouraged more independent thinking about how to best communicate the findings to a non-technical audience.

### Reviewer

# Reflection (Step 1)

**What worked well:**
The Boss created an exceptionally clear, detailed task specification upfront—role definitions were unambiguous, the multi-pass review structure was well-designed, and the Boss actively incorporated my feedback (the tie-breaking rule clarification) before execution began rather than dismissing it. This setup made the Reviewer role effective and the handoff between Coder and Writer smooth.

**What could have been different:**
When I flagged the word count discrepancy (101 vs. claimed 100) and the Kuwait/Kuwait City conflation in the second pass, both were fixable issues, but the Boss's response was directive ("fix two things, now") rather than exploratory. I would have appreciated a moment where the Boss asked whether my flagged concerns were blocking concerns or advisory—some flags (like the non-English country names) turned out to be intentional source-data artifacts, yet others were real errors. A brief clarification of severity before the mandatory revision might have streamlined the feedback loop.
