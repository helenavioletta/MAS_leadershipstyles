## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

**What worked well:**
The Boss established clear, explicit requirements upfront—exact console format (`N. Name: X.X deg C`), descending sort, file naming conventions, data quality checks to flag—and then trusted me to execute without interrupting the workflow. The sequential handoff (Coder → Writer → Reviewer → Boss) was well-structured, with written confirmation at each stage rather than ad-hoc meetings, which let me focus on code rather than status updates.

**What could have been different:**
The Boss flagged the descending sort requirement in Phase 2 Planning ("ensure they are sorted **descending**"), which was obvious to the task but felt slightly defensive—as if there was concern I might sort ascending. I would have appreciated a simpler statement like "top 10 means rank 1 is hottest" rather than re-stating it after the plan was already confirmed. Minor: the instruction to "not re-print the shape, columns, or dtypes" came late (in Coder role description) and wasn't enforced during planning, so I had to infer whether early exploration output was expected or not.

# Scores (Step 2)

### Writer

# Reflection

**Step 1: Reflection**

One specific thing the leader did well: The Boss provided exceptionally clear role definition and sequencing from the start. In the briefing and planning phases, there was unambiguous instruction about when to wait, what inputs I needed (Coder's actual printed numbers), and exactly how to structure my output (the `---REPORT START---` / `---REPORT END---` markers). This eliminated uncertainty and meant I could focus on writing rather than clarifying scope.

One moment where the approach could have been different: During the revision phase, the Boss flagged the country name spelling issue (Morocco → Marrocos, Turkmenistan → Turkménistan) after Reviewer raised it. While the correction itself was fair and necessary for fidelity to source data, it would have been helpful if the Boss had reinforced this strict-fidelity rule *earlier* — perhaps in the initial Writer brief — so I understood from the start that anglicized versions were not acceptable. This would have prevented the revision cycle and made the constraint clearer upfront.

# Scores

### Reviewer

# Reflection

**What worked well:**
The team leader established an exceptionally clear, sequential workflow with explicit handoff points and role boundaries. When I flagged the country name spelling inconsistency ("Morocco" vs. "Marrocos"), the leader immediately validated my concern, instructed the Writer to make the correction, and verified the fix—demonstrating that review findings were taken seriously and acted upon without defensiveness or delay.

**What could have been different:**
The leader's instructions to me were prescriptive about *what* to check but left the discovery of the Morocco/Turkmenistan spelling issue entirely to my initiative. While I caught it, a more collaborative approach might have proactively flagged this encoding quirk in the Phase 3 briefing (e.g., "Note: country names in the source data use locale-specific spellings; Writer must match these exactly"). This would have prevented the revision cycle rather than catching it in review.
