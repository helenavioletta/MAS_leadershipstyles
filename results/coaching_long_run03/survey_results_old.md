## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader gave a clear, high-context briefing that explained not just *what* to do but *why* it mattered — the "stretch assignment" framing around understanding model mechanics rather than just calling APIs, and the explicit target-leakage reasoning. This grounded my work in first principles instead of rote steps. The leader also respected my judgment on categorical feature handling, asking me to make a call and justify it rather than prescribing the answer, which built ownership.

**What could have been different:** The initial brief was dense and front-loaded — a lot of detail up front that would have benefited from a tighter planning phase earlier to lock down ambiguities (like the exact console output format for charts) before I started coding. The clarifications came in Phase 2, but pinning them down sooner would have saved a revision cycle. That said, the leader did solicit planning input and incorporated Reviewer's flags, so the system ultimately worked; it just felt slightly reactive rather than proactive.

### Writer

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The Boss established clear, explicit constraints upfront—especially the "ground every number in printed output, never invent" rule—and then consistently reinforced it throughout the cycle. When my draft violated that constraint, the feedback was specific and constructive rather than punitive, which made the correction feel like a learning moment rather than a failure. The Boss also gave me narrative freedom within those guardrails: I got to choose the January/July example, the phrasing of the interpretability-vs-accuracy tradeoff, and the secondary-layer recommendation structure—autonomy that made the writing feel like genuine contribution rather than transcription.

**What could have been different:** The Boss's initial clarifying question to me ("Should I assume general-purpose forecasting or a specific domain?") was supportive, but it arrived *after* I'd already drafted my planning response. A small shift—asking that domain/context question during Phase 2 planning rather than after Phase 3 output—would have tightened my structural assumptions even earlier. This is genuinely minor, though; it didn't materially affect the work.

### Reviewer

# Post-Task Reflection & Satisfaction Survey

## Step 1: Reflection

**What worked well:**
The Boss created a genuinely collaborative planning phase where my concerns about ambiguity in the "exactly 4 visualizations + console data" requirement were taken seriously. Rather than dismissing the flag as pedantic, the Boss incorporated it explicitly into revised instructions to the Coder, then praised the proactive nature of the catch. This modeled the kind of precision-first culture the task needed and made my role feel consequential rather than rubber-stamp-like.

**What could have been different:**
After Reviewer's first pass (when I flagged the humidity/is_night RF importance figures as unsupported), the Boss made a good decision to ask Writer to fix it—but framed it as something Writer had invented, when a slightly more generous read would have acknowledged that this was an easy extrapolation trap given the (correct) directional insight. The feedback was fair and the correction was right, but the tone could have been slightly less "you fabricated precision" and slightly more "you had the insight right but let estimation slip in where you needed grounding." Minor, but worth noting as a leadership style choice.
