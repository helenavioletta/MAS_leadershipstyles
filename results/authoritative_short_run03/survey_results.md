## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

During the briefing, the Boss clearly defined my role and scope—load data, compute four rankings, print them in a specific format, generate four charts—without dictating *how* I should structure the code or which libraries to use. This autonomy allowed me to design a clean pipeline that worked for me. When the Reviewer flagged an issue with the Writer's arithmetic claim about temperature gaps, the Boss didn't blame the Coder or demand a rewrite of my outputs; instead, he treated my console data as the reliable audit trail and asked the Writer to fix the claim. That reinforced that the team trusted my work and that precision downstream came from accuracy at the source.

# Scores (Step 2)

### Writer

# Reflection on My Experience as Writer

**Specific action that shaped my work:**
The Boss's instruction to focus on *what the numbers reveal* rather than explaining methodology was critical—it reframed my entire approach from teaching the audience about averages/maxima to telling them an interesting story grounded in concrete data. This freed me to write more compellingly within the tight word budget.

**Decision/interaction that stood out:**
When the Reviewer flagged my "3–4°C" claim as unsupported, the Boss immediately offered two concrete revision options (simplify or anchor to a specific example) rather than just sending me back to "fix it." This transformed a correction into a collaborative upgrade—I chose option 2 not because I was told to, but because I could see it would strengthen the narrative. That kind of guidance-with-autonomy made the revision feel like improvement, not punishment.
