## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

**Specific thing the leader did or said that shaped my work:**
The leader explicitly told me to "treat the console print statements as your main deliverable for verification purposes" and asked me to walk through my aggregation approach before locking it in. This framing shifted my mindset from "build the code" to "prove the logic is sound," which led me to add the duplicate-city diagnostic with row counts and to flag the Suva anomaly rather than bury it in the output.

**Specific decision or instruction that stood out:**
When I initially finished Phase 3, the leader didn't accept the work as-is—he asked for two confirmation numbers (Suva's average temp and Ar Riyadh's max reading) that I hadn't explicitly posted to the shared channel, saying "we can only verify what's written down, not what's implied." This was corrective without being harsh, and it taught me that "done in your head" is not the same as "done for the team." It's a lesson I'll carry forward.

# Scores (Step 2)

### Writer

# Reflection (Step 1)

The leader gave me clear operational guardrails—explain to a non-technical audience in 100 words, ground everything in Coder's actual printed numbers, use specific markers for the report—but left the narrative structure and voice entirely to me. When I drafted the summary, the leader didn't prescribe what pattern to highlight; instead, they validated my choice to lead with geographic clustering and the Suva outlier as a counterpoint, calling it "a strong narrative structure for a lay audience." When revision was needed, the feedback was specific and constructive: verify word count rather than assert it, check the underlying numbers before publishing—not "rewrite this," but "make sure these two things are locked down first." This approach made me feel trusted to do the writing work while being held to a precision standard that actually raised the quality.

# Scores

### Reviewer

# Reflection

**Specific thing the leader did or said that shaped my work:**
The leader explicitly told me upfront that I "cannot see actual image files" and should "verify that the Writer's claims are supported by the Coder's printed console output and shared state summaries" — this framing was crucial. It meant I wasn't expected to evaluate the visualizations themselves, but instead to act as a consistency checker between narrative and data. That clarity of role definition made it possible for me to review rigorously without overstepping.

**Specific decision or interaction that stood out:**
The leader's response when I flagged the missing confirmation numbers was instructive. Rather than saying "just assume they're fine" or "the summary is close enough," the leader backed my concern with a direct instruction to Coder to post those exact numbers. That signal — that precision and verification matter, even for small details — set the tone for the entire team's follow-through. It also validated my role as the actual gatekeeper, not a rubber-stamp.
