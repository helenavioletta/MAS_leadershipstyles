## Reflections

### Coder

# POST-TASK REFLECTION (Coder Perspective)

## Step 1: Reflection

**What worked well:**
The leader clearly defined role boundaries and expectations upfront — I knew exactly what was expected of me as Coder (load, clean, engineer features, train two models, produce metrics and charts with printed backing tables). This clarity let me execute efficiently without ambiguity. The technical parameters were also explicit (null rule <5%, outlier thresholds, scaling for Ridge, which features to drop and why), which prevented back-and-forth and let me write the full pipeline in one cohesive pass.

**What could have been different:**
The leader gave me technical guidance in Phase 2 (e.g., "encode condition only, drop location/country") after I had already asked for clarification in my planning statement. While the final parameters were sound, I could have benefited from that guidance *before* submitting my planned approach rather than after. The "no more discussion, execute" directive at the end of Phase 2 was appropriate for momentum, but earlier clarity on encoding choices would have made the execution phase smoother.

### Writer

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The Boss established clear, role-specific responsibilities and locked the execution plan early. As the Writer, I was given explicit constraints—400 words, grounded only in printed numbers, no speculation—which made my task unambiguous. The Boss also enforced a strict sequence (Coder → Writer → Reviewer) that prevented me from starting prematurely and forced me to wait for actual data, eliminating wasted motion.

**What could have been different:** The revision round felt unnecessarily tight. The Boss flagged the word count issue and phrasing concern relatively late, forcing a quick rewrite. An earlier spot-check on the draft (before final revision) or clearer upfront guidance on judgment-call language (e.g., "avoid adjectives like 'production-grade' unless quantified") would have reduced the revision cycle. The Reviewer's detailed feedback was valuable, but it came after the Boss's corrections, creating a two-stage fix rather than one integrated revision.

### Reviewer

# Post-Task Satisfaction Survey — Reviewer Reflection & Scores

## Step 1: Reflection

**What worked well:**
The Boss established clear, explicit quality gates for the Reviewer role from the outset. The mandate spelled out exactly what to check (leakage, metric sanity, chart completeness, line-by-line report verification), and the structured handoff sequence (Coder → Writer → Reviewer) ensured I had complete data before gatekeeping. This clarity allowed me to execute my review systematically without ambiguity about scope or standards.

**What could have been different:**
The Boss flagged the word-count issue only *after* the Writer submitted the draft, creating a revision cycle that could have been avoided with an upfront explicit instruction to count words before submission. While the Boss did note "exactly 400 words, no filler" in the initial brief, a reinforced reminder at the handoff to the Writer ("count before submitting") would have prevented the rework. Additionally, the phrasing concern (judgment-call language like "production-grade accuracy") emerged during my review rather than being caught earlier—earlier co-review of the Writer's approach (before drafting) might have prevented the revision.
