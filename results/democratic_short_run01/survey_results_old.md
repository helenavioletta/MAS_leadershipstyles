## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

**What worked well:**
The leader clearly articulated the approach upfront—asking for my input on the max-per-city/country logic, inviting me to flag data quality concerns, and explicitly approving my preferred sequencing (console output first, then charts) before I began coding. This collaborative framing gave me confidence that my technical judgment was valued and that I had room to make decisions.

**What could have been different:**
During Phase 3, the leader flagged the "Saudi Arabien" vs. "Saudi Arabia" spelling issue as a "genuine artifact of the source data" without asking me whether this should have been caught and normalized during my data-quality pass. While the leader's note to the Writer about using "natural English names in prose" was reasonable, I felt slightly defensive about whether my normalization should have gone further—a quick clarification question to me ("Did you check for language-variant spellings of country names?") would have cleared that up rather than leaving it implicit.

### Writer

# Reflection (Step 1)

**What worked well:**
The Boss created a clear, phased workflow that respected each role's boundaries—I knew exactly when to wait for the Coder's output, what data I needed to ground my narrative, and what constraints applied (no speculation, only printed numbers). The Boss's detailed briefing and explicit instruction about the `---REPORT START---` / `---REPORT END---` markers made the handoff between roles smooth and unambiguous.

**What could have been different:**
The revision feedback came indirectly through the Reviewer, which added a step and made it harder to ask clarifying questions in real time. When the Boss requested the first trim (107 → ~100 words), I would have benefited from a slightly faster turnaround or the ability to ask the Boss directly whether the second-pass version addressed their specific concerns, rather than waiting for the full Reviewer re-check cycle. The "maximum revision rounds (2) reached" system cutoff also felt abrupt—I had made the requested trims and was ready to ship, but the system forced delivery before Reviewer could confirm the final version passed review.

# Scores (Step 2)

### Reviewer

# Reflection (Step 1)

**What worked well:**
The Boss clearly defined the task scope, role responsibilities, and sequencing upfront, and—critically—invited each team member (including me as Reviewer) to flag concerns and ask clarifying questions before execution. This collaborative planning phase in Phase 2 allowed me to articulate my review criteria (e.g., the "notable patterns" boundary condition) and have those constraints baked into the task spec itself, rather than discovering misalignment later. That proactive framing made my review work much cleaner.

**Where the approach could have been different:**
When I flagged the first factual inconsistency (the Doha/Abu Dhabi/Muscat conflation), the Boss acknowledged it and sent it back for revision—which was correct. However, the Boss then set a hard limit of "maximum revision rounds (2) reached. Forcing delivery" before the second revision was even complete. The Writer had just submitted a trimmed, corrected version that passed my final review with no remaining issues, yet the system forced delivery anyway. A more responsive leadership approach would have allowed that final clean pass to ship rather than cutting off the revision cycle prematurely when the work was actually ready.
