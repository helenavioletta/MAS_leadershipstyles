## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

**Specific action or statement from the leader:**
The leader issued a single, comprehensive briefing upfront with explicit specifications (data location, target column, feature engineering constraints, model choices, output format), then stated "No hand-holding beyond this message" and "First pass should be correct and complete." This set a clear expectation: I owned the execution end-to-end, but the requirements were non-negotiable.

**Specific decision or interaction that stood out:**
When the Reviewer flagged a procedural requirement—that I must state explicitly the categorical encoding decision for `country`/`location_name` with rationale—the leader incorporated this into a final confirmation message ("Non-negotiable — Reviewer will check for it first") rather than leaving it ambiguous. This showed the leader was willing to tighten unclear specs based on the team's input, but once locked, expected delivery to match exactly.

### Writer

# Reflection (Step 1)

The team leader's most impactful move was establishing an explicit, gated sequence upfront: Coder → Reviewer audit → Writer draft → Reviewer final check. This prevented me from speculating or drafting against unverified data, which would have wasted effort. The leader also gave me a clear constraint (400 words, grounded only in console output, no invented statistics) and trusted me to execute within it—no prescriptive hand-holding on *how* to structure the narrative, only what the narrative must contain. When the Reviewer flagged the speculative precip_mm sentence, the leader did not rewrite it for me; instead, they gave me two concrete, data-grounded replacement options and let me choose. This balance of clear constraint + autonomy shaped the work: I had guardrails, not a straitjacket.

# Scores (Step 2)

### Reviewer

# Reflection

**Specific action that shaped my work:**
The Boss issued a non-negotiable requirement for the Coder to state categorical encoding decisions explicitly in console output—a requirement that was then baked into the execution plan with clear accountability language ("Non-negotiable — Reviewer will check for it first"). This targeted instruction meant I had a concrete, verifiable criterion to audit against rather than subjective judgment calls.

**Specific decision that stood out:**
When I flagged the speculative language in the Writer's report (the "rainy seasons in tropics vs. dry seasons in deserts" claim), the Boss immediately accepted my concern as valid, provided two concrete revision options grounded in the data, and issued a single-pass fix instruction to the Writer. The Boss did not defend the original text, did not ask me to "reconsider," and did not iterate endlessly—the decision was fast and decisive, and the Writer executed it cleanly on the first revision.
