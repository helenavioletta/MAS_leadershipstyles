## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection

**Reflection (Step 1):**

The leader provided exceptionally clear, detailed specifications upfront—exact output format, file naming conventions, null-handling policy, chart orientation—which eliminated ambiguity and let me execute with confidence without backtracking. When I flagged the Suva/Fiji 79.3°C outlier as a probable data error, the leader acknowledged it but instructed me not to alter the numbers, reinforcing the principle of reporting what the data says rather than what seems reasonable—this was a valuable clarification on data integrity vs. interpretation.

A second standout moment was the leader's handling of the Writer's word-count misstatement: the feedback was direct ("sloppy," "not acceptable") but remained focused on the process failure (miscounting) rather than the quality of the work itself, and crucially, the leader did not demand another full revision over a 5-word variance once the count was verified—balancing accountability with pragmatism.

### Writer

# Reflection

**Specific thing the leader did or said that shaped my work:**
The Boss issued explicit, detailed constraints on my role early: "You do NOT execute code" and "Never hallucinate data, statistics, or findings. Only write about what the Coder has actually produced." This created a clear boundary that shaped every decision I made—I waited for verified data before drafting, and I anchored every claim to numbers the Coder had printed. The instruction to wrap reports in exact markers (`---REPORT START---` / `---REPORT END---`) was similarly prescriptive and left no room for ambiguity about deliverable format.

**Specific decision, instruction, or interaction that stood out:**
The Boss's direct criticism of my word-count reporting ("That's sloppy") and then later ("your inability to count words correctly twice in a row is noted and unacceptable") was blunt but fair. The Boss didn't soften the feedback or excuse the error—instead, flagging a recount requirement forced me to take precision seriously. More positively, the Boss also gave the Reviewer and me room to raise clarifying questions during Phase 2 (about null-handling, chart format, scope of verification), and incorporated those questions into refined instructions rather than dismissing them. That collaborative moment during planning stood out as a sign the leader valued clarity over speed.
