## Reflections

### Coder

# POST-TASK SURVEY — CODER REFLECTION & SCORING

## Step 1: Reflection

**What worked well:**
The Boss provided exceptionally clear requirements upfront—exact output format, file naming conventions, null-handling policy, and chart specifications were all explicit before Phase 3 began. This removed ambiguity and allowed me to execute confidently without rework. When I had clarifying questions in Phase 2, the Boss answered them directly and decisively, unblocking the task immediately.

**What could have been different:**
The task structure created a one-way delivery pattern where I executed code in isolation and then had no further input once the Reviewer and Writer took over. While this mirrors real-world handoffs, I had no visibility into whether my console output was actually readable or useful to downstream work until the Reviewer's verification. A quick checkpoint ("does the printed format work for you?") before final submission would have given me confidence that my output met unstated expectations around clarity.

### Writer

# Reflection on My Experience as the Writer

**What worked well:**
The Boss established crystal-clear role boundaries and sequencing. By explicitly stating "Writer, stand by. You get nothing until Coder delivers printed data. Do not draft anything yet," the leader prevented me from writing unsupported narratives and ensured all my work was grounded in verified output. This structure eliminated guesswork and made the writing task straightforward: use only what the Coder printed, nothing more.

**What could have been different:**
The Boss's feedback on my word-count reporting was harsh ("That's sloppy," "unacceptable") when the actual discrepancy was minor and did not affect the deliverable. The underlying issue—that I misreported my own count—was fair to flag, but the tone and repeated emphasis on accountability for a 5–10 word overrun on a ~100-word target felt disproportionate. More constructive framing ("Recount before submitting; precision matters in reporting") would have been equally clear without the personal criticism. Additionally, the Boss could have acknowledged that the Reviewer caught and corrected the error before delivery, which mitigated the risk.

### Reviewer

# Reflection

**What worked well:**
The Boss established a crystal-clear task specification upfront—exact output format, column names, file naming conventions, and sequencing—which eliminated ambiguity and prevented rework. When I flagged the null-handling policy ambiguity in Phase 2, the leader responded immediately with a one-sentence clarification rather than deflecting, which kept the task on track.

**What could have been different:**
The Boss's feedback to the Writer about word-count miscounts was dismissive ("sloppy," "unacceptable") rather than coaching-oriented. While accuracy matters, the tone did not invite the Writer to improve the process—it felt like reproach rather than collaborative problem-solving. Additionally, the Boss could have caught the "Saudi Arabien" vs. "Saudi Arabia" naming issue earlier by previewing the data labels before handing output to the Writer, rather than flagging it as a Writer error after the fact.
