## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

**What worked well:**
The Boss clearly defined my role and responsibilities upfront, gave me autonomy to choose my technical approach (horizontal vs. vertical charts, data validation strategy), and accepted my outputs without requesting unnecessary rework. The planning phase included detailed specifications without micromanaging *how* I'd implement them—I could decide on grouping logic, chart libraries, and data quality checks independently.

**What could have been different:**
In the briefing, the Boss didn't ask me to confirm feasibility or flag potential blockers before Phase 3 began (e.g., "Do you need the file path validated?" or "Should we discuss edge cases like missing values upfront?"). This wouldn't have changed the outcome, but early clarification would have reduced the small ambiguity about city-name encoding in the raw data that the Reviewer later flagged.

### Writer

# Reflection (Step 1)

**One specific thing that worked well:**
The Boss established crystal-clear role definitions upfront—I knew exactly when to wait for the Coder's outputs and what to ground my writing in (actual numbers, never invented figures). This clarity meant I could focus on the narrative rather than second-guessing scope or boundaries. The structured phases (Briefing → Planning → Coding → Writing → Review → Revision → Delivery) also kept the workflow transparent and prevented bottlenecks.

**One moment where the approach could have been different:**
When the Reviewer flagged the word count and clarity issues in my first draft, the feedback came only after I'd submitted—ideally, a mid-draft checkpoint or a brief style guide before I wrote might have caught those issues earlier and saved a revision cycle. The feedback itself was precise and actionable, but a "Writer's toolkit" or brief guidance on tone/conciseness before drafting could have prevented the need for revision altogether.

# Scores (Step 2)

### Reviewer

# Reflection (Step 1)

**What worked well:** The team leader created a clear, detailed execution plan in Phase 2 that explicitly addressed my concerns about chart verification and city-name ambiguity — rather than dismissing them, the leader incorporated my suggestions (chart metadata printout, city ambiguity handling clarification) into the task instructions. This gave me concrete, verifiable criteria to audit against.

**What could have been different:** During Phase 6 revision requests, the leader framed Coder's country-name issue as "confirm and document" rather than "investigate whether this is a problem to solve." While the outcome was transparent documentation (which was appropriate), the leader could have offered more guidance upfront about whether source-data language mixing was expected or required escalation — this would have reduced ambiguity about the scope of Coder's revision work.
