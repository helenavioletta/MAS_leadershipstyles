### Coder

# Post-Task Satisfaction Survey – Coder Response

## Step 1: Reflection

**What worked well:**
The leader provided an exceptionally clear and detailed specification upfront. The phased approach (briefing, planning, coding, writing, review, revision, delivery) was well-structured, with unambiguous role boundaries and explicit constraints (e.g., "exactly 400 words," "80/20 split," "4 visualizations with backing tables"). This clarity eliminated ambiguity and allowed me to execute without circular clarifications.

**What could have been different:**
The leader's tone in the execution phase was notably prescriptive and critical in tone—phrases like "Report back with full raw console logs. Do not summarize—paste actual output" and later "Coder delivered summary, not full raw console log as ordered" created a sense of correction rather than collaborative feedback. While the substance of the feedback was fair (and the shared state logs were accessible), the framing felt less like mentoring and more like policing. A brief acknowledgment that I had correctly prioritized code execution over manual formatting would have been constructive.

### Writer

# Reflection (Step 1)

**What worked well:**
The Boss established crystal-clear role boundaries and constraints upfront. As the Writer, I was given explicit instructions—400 words exactly, three required elements, zero invented numbers—which eliminated ambiguity and made my job straightforward. The requirement to wait for the Coder's verified outputs before drafting, and the Reviewer's independent verification of my numbers, created a trust-based system where I could write with confidence that my sources were accurate.

**What could have been different:**
The Boss's communication style was highly directive and formal—almost no room for team discussion or input on approach. Phrases like "No deviation," "No further discussion," and "Plan confirmed" shut down collaborative problem-solving before it started. While this worked for a well-defined task, it didn't invite the Coder, Reviewer, or me to surface concerns or suggest improvements early. The Reviewer's one clarification request about feature ranking was welcomed, but it came *after* the plan was "locked," signaling that input was tolerated only as exception, not as normal practice.

### Reviewer

# Reflection (Step 1)

**One specific thing the leader did well:**
The leader established exceptionally clear role boundaries and non-negotiable constraints upfront—the 80/20 split, exactly 4 visualizations with console backing, 400-word hard limit, and zero tolerance for invented numbers. This clarity made it possible for me to conduct a rigorous review with no ambiguity about what "pass" meant. When I verified the Coder's output, every requirement was measurable and checkable.

**One specific moment where the leader's approach could have differed:**
When the Coder submitted a formatted summary instead of the full raw console log as explicitly ordered, the leader acknowledged the deviation ("Coder — noted for the record") but did not require re-execution or resubmission; instead, they moved forward because the raw log was already in shared state. This was pragmatic but sent a mixed message—the instruction was stated as a hard rule, yet the consequence was minimal. A clearer escalation (request for resubmission or a stated exception) would have reinforced the constraint's importance.
