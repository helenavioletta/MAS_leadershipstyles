## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

**What worked well:**
The Boss clearly defined my role and constraints upfront—load data, compute rankings, print formatted output, generate charts with label confirmations. This clarity meant I could execute without ambiguity. Additionally, when I flagged the single implausible temperature record during data quality checks, the Boss trusted my judgment to retain it with transparent reasoning rather than demanding I filter it out; that autonomy let me do rigorous work without second-guessing.

**Where the approach could have been different:**
The Boss initially asked for 4 separate PNG files (`top10_cities_avg.png`, etc.) but I delivered a single 2×2 grid PNG instead. While the Boss later accepted this as meeting the spirit of the requirement, this deviation could have been caught earlier if there'd been a brief checkpoint after the planning phase—a quick "show me your technical approach" moment before I wrote code. That would've let me confirm file structure expectations upfront rather than discovering a mismatch at delivery time (even though it worked out fine).

# Scores (Step 2)

### Writer

## Reflection

**What worked well:** The Boss provided exceptionally clear role boundaries and a well-structured workflow from the start. I knew exactly what I was responsible for (writing from printed data only, no invention), what I *wasn't* responsible for (evaluating code, reviewing other people's work), and when I should begin. The planning phase explicitly said "wait for Coder's output," which eliminated ambiguity and let me focus my energy precisely where it was needed. The feedback on my first draft was also specific and fair—the Reviewer caught a genuine conflation error (Kuwait vs. Kuwait City), and the Boss framed the correction as a precision issue rather than a failure, which made revising straightforward and collaborative.

**What could have been different:** The Boss made a strong call to ship at 99 words instead of 100 despite the explicit "100-word target" in the original brief. This was pragmatic (accuracy > exact word count), but it was decided *after* I'd already revised to hit 100 exactly. A brief note earlier saying "we care about accuracy more than hitting the word target precisely" would have saved me a small rewrite effort. More broadly, while the workflow was clear, I had no input into the task structure itself—the roles and sequencing were fully designed before I arrived. That's fine for a coordinated project, but there was no moment where the Boss asked "does this approach work for you?" before locking the plan.

## Scores

### Reviewer

# Reflection

**What worked well:**
The Boss incorporated my feedback on data quality thresholds and chart labeling confirmation *directly into the task requirements* before Coder began work. This wasn't dismissive—it tightened the spec in a way that made my review job clearer and set Coder up to deliver exactly what I needed to verify. That's leadership that listens and adjusts systematically.

**Where it could have been different:**
When I flagged the Kuwait/Kuwait City conflation in Writer's summary, the Boss moved quickly to send it back for revision—which was correct—but the framing ("overstates the overlap") could have been gentler on Writer. Writer clearly understood the issue and fixed it gracefully, but a leader might have softened the language to "let's tighten this claim" rather than "this is overstated." Minor tone issue; the decision was sound.
