## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection

**Specific thing the leader did or said that shaped my work:**
In Phase 2, the leader explicitly told me "No fixed 'right' way to do your part — use your judgment and expertise" and later reinforced this by saying any Reviewer concerns were "no pressure to treat this as a rigid checklist" but rather "make explicit what you're already doing." This gave me confidence to own my technical decisions (feature exclusion, model choice, residual statistics) while still being transparent about them.

**One specific decision or interaction that stood out:**
When the Reviewer raised five methodological questions in Phase 2, the leader framed them not as "you must do this" but as "no pressure, but folding these in will make your output stronger" — and then explicitly said "Totally your call on exact wording/format." This felt collaborative rather than prescriptive. The leader also explicitly encouraged me to talk directly with Writer and Reviewer if something needed clarification, rather than routing everything through them. That trust and permission to self-organize within the team was striking.

### Writer

# Reflection (Step 1)

The team leader set a clear, flexible approach in the opening briefing that laid out what I needed to do (write a ~400-word report grounded in Coder's actual outputs) without dictating the exact structure or tone—they explicitly framed it as "a starting point, not a rulebook" and encouraged me to adapt if I saw a better path. During the planning phase, when the Reviewer raised methodological concerns, the leader validated those points without turning them into rigid requirements for me; instead, they asked the Coder to "make explicit what you're already doing" so I'd have clean data to work with, and then explicitly told me "love the structure you've sketched" and affirmed my commitment to grounding findings in real numbers rather than filler. After I submitted my draft, the leader gave specific, genuine praise—noting exactly what worked (the explanation of *why* Ridge fails, the interaction-effects callback, the logical flow)—without notes for revision because the work was solid, and that straightforward assessment felt earned rather than flattering.

**Reflection (Step 1):**

The Boss's approach to my pre-flight review concerns was exemplary. Rather than dismissing or deferring my methodological questions, the Boss explicitly validated them ("these are great, sharp points") and encouraged Coder to incorporate them, framing them as strengthening the output rather than adding burden. This signal—that quality concerns raised early are valued and actionable—shaped how I conducted my final review: I reviewed with confidence that the team had the discipline to address issues before they reached me, which meant my review could focus on genuine verification rather than firefighting.

It reframed documentation not as overhead but as accountability and transparency, which allowed me to review the methodology cleanly (random seed logged, feature leakage explicitly justified, identical train sets confirmed) rather than having to infer intent or ask follow-up questions.

**Reflection (Step 2):**

The Boss's decision to allow direct communication between Coder and Writer ("feel free to talk directly to each other if something needs a quick clarification") without requiring all messages to route through leadership built psychological safety and trust in the team. This created an environment where quality could be maintained collaboratively rather than through gatekeeping, which made my review phase feel like a genuine quality check rather than a bottleneck or audit.
