## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader explicitly asked for my input on approach during Phase 2, treating the data-quality concerns and ambiguous decisions (duplicate city names, country normalization) as genuine decision points rather than prescribing solutions. When I flagged the >5 duplicate-city threshold in Phase 3, the leader didn't dismiss it or tell me to "just pick something"—instead, they explained the reasoning against silent data-dropping (Option 2) and locked in a transparent choice (composite labels). This gave me confidence that the work was collaborative rather than me just executing a predetermined plan.

**What could have been different:** The leader could have given me more autonomy in Phase 3 regarding the country-name normalization. I flagged 4 variants and recommended normalizing them, but the leader then asked me to execute that exact fix rather than letting me decide the scope independently. It was a reasonable call in hindsight (the variants *were* confirmed encoding issues), but it shifted me slightly back into "execute my decision" mode after inviting my input earlier. A lighter touch—e.g., "normalize the ones you're confident about, document your choices"—would have extended the collaborative framing.

---

### Writer

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The Boss clearly established my role upfront — wait for actual numbers, don't invent findings, ground the summary in real data — and then actually let me do that job without interference. When I drafted the summary, the Reviewer flagged one optional gap (Fiji Islands country mention), but the Boss explicitly validated my word-count constraint and didn't force a revision. I was trusted to make narrative choices (like which figures to highlight) while staying grounded in the data Coder provided.

**What could have been different:** The planning phase was thorough, but the Boss could have given me slightly more proactive guidance on *tone* or *emphasis* before I drafted. For instance, I made a choice to lead with the avg-vs-max distinction because I thought that was the most important story for a lay reader, but I wasn't explicitly told whether that was the right priority or if regional clustering should come first. A quick "what's the main insight you want readers to walk away with?" from the Boss during planning would have aligned my narrative strategy earlier and possibly saved a revision cycle (though in this case there wasn't one).

---

### Reviewer

# Reflection

**What worked well:** The leader explicitly invited the Reviewer's input at the planning stage ("Reviewer — anything you want us to watch out for?"), incorporated my suggestion about the duplicate-city decision rule into the formal plan, and then consistently referred back to that rule when ambiguous situations arose (Coder paused, Boss cited the pre-agreed framework rather than making an ad-hoc call). This prevented scope creep and kept the team aligned.

**What could have been different:** During the country-name normalization decision, the Boss framed it as a clear factual matter ("these are confirmed encoding duplicates, not judgment calls like the city situation") and unilaterally decided to normalize without explicitly re-checking with the team whether the Reviewer had concerns about that scope of change. While the decision itself was sound, the framing as "not the same as the city situation" shut down a follow-up discussion that might have been useful. A 30-second "Reviewer, does this feel like the right boundary to you?" would have reinforced collaborative decision-making rather than just relying on prior agreement.
