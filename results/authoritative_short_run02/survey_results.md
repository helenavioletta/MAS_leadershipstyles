## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection (Step 1)

During the briefing, the leader gave me clear technical autonomy—explicitly saying "How you structure the code, what libraries you lean on... that's entirely up to you. I trust your judgment"—which immediately established confidence in my ability to decide the implementation path. When I asked three clarification questions in Phase 2 (rounding precision, handling ties, data quality scope), the leader answered decisively without over-constraining my choices, then said "you're clear to proceed," signaling trust rather than oversight.

A standout moment came after I delivered the code output: instead of nitpicking the analysis or asking me to re-run things, the leader reviewed the deliverable against the *vision* (would a non-expert understand this?) and gave me constructive feedback on one observation ("the Ar Riyadh tie is confusing as written") while affirming the overall foundation ("solid, verifiable, and complete"). This balanced validation with honest critique, making the feedback feel purposeful rather than arbitrary.

### Writer

# Reflection (Step 1)

The team leader's briefing in Phase 1 was exceptionally clear and goal-oriented—they articulated the vision ("a non-expert should walk away with a correct, clear understanding") upfront and repeatedly, which gave my writing work a true north star rather than just a format checklist. Most significantly, when the Reviewer flagged the Kuwait/Kuwait City conflation in my first draft, the leader didn't just relay the feedback; they diagnosed *why* it mattered (planting a wrong idea in readers' heads) and then explicitly trusted me to find the fix myself by offering choices ("either clarify the two are distinct cities, or pick a clearer example") rather than dictating a single solution.

This balance—crystal-clear expectations + autonomy in execution + honest pushback on errors—made the revision cycle feel collaborative rather than punitive, and the final summary is stronger because I had to think through the Abu Dhabi example rather than have it handed to me.
