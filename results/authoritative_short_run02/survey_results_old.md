## Reflections

### Coder

# Reflection

**What worked well:**
The leader set exceptionally clear expectations upfront — the briefing spelled out the exact output format ("1. Paris: 25.2 deg C"), the data path, the columns to use, and the role boundaries. This eliminated ambiguity and let me focus on clean code rather than guessing what "good" looked like. The leader also trusted my judgment on technical choices (libraries, structure) and didn't prescribe the solution, which is rare and valuable.

**Where approach could differ:**
The leader asked clarifying questions back to me in Phase 2 (rounding, ties, data quality depth) but then answered them himself in the wrap-up rather than having me execute and report back. It's a minor point — the answers were sensible — but it meant I didn't get to run the data quality check, find issues, and report them myself. The leader assumed cleanliness; I could have added more credibility to the foundation by doing and documenting the inspection work. More autonomy in the discovery phase would have made the evidence trail even stronger.

### Writer

# Reflection (Step 1)

**One thing that worked well:**
The leader established a clear, role-specific workflow upfront—laying out exactly what the Writer needed (printed console numbers, not visual descriptions) and *when* to proceed (after Coder delivered). This eliminated ambiguity and let me focus on narrative craft rather than guessing what I should be doing. The revision feedback was also direct and specific: "Issue 1 conflates Kuwait/Kuwait City; fix it with Abu Dhabi instead," not vague notes like "clarity needed."

**One moment that could have been different:**
When the leader sent the revision back with the Abu Dhabi suggestion already pre-decided, there was little room for me to propose an alternative approach (e.g., dropping the avg-vs-peak example entirely, or framing it differently). The fix was good, but the instruction felt more like "do this" than "here's the problem, what do you think?" I would have valued a moment to propose solutions before being handed one.

### Reviewer

# Reflection (Step 1)

**What worked well:** The Boss was exceptionally clear in setting up the review process. They explicitly told me what to verify (console output vs. narrative claims, spec compliance, word count), explained *why* it mattered (non-experts shouldn't walk away confused), and gave me authority to flag real problems. When I raised the Kuwait/Kuwait City conflation issue, the Boss immediately recognized it as a material problem — not a nitpick — and sent the work back for targeted revision. That validation made the feedback meaningful and showed the Boss was listening, not just checking a box.

**Where it could have differed:** The Boss made an assumption in their initial review of the Writer's first draft that the Kuwait/Kuwait City distinction was "worth double-checking" but didn't flag it as a definite problem themselves — they deferred to me. While this is appropriate delegation, a sharper initial read by the Boss might have caught it before it reached my desk, saving a revision cycle. Alternatively, the Boss could have asked me earlier (e.g., during Phase 2) whether I had concerns about the Writer's approach to handling avg-vs-peak comparisons before the draft landed.
